Collectivist selfPrioritizes group needs, loyalty, and harmony.

Individual focusPrioritizing the person.
Group focusPrioritizing the group.
IndependenceMaking choices with less influence from others.
InterdependenceRelying on and considering others.
Self-expressionShowing personal ideas and preferences.
Social obligationResponsibilities toward the group.

Lesson 11

The Sexual Self: Development of the Secondary Sex

Sexual selfUnderstanding sexual growth, changes, health, attraction, identity, and values.
Sexual developmentA lifelong process beginning at conception.
Sigmund FreudFounder of the psychosexual stages of development.
Psychosexual stagesFive stages: oral, anal, phallic, latency, and genital.
Erogenous zoneBody area where pleasure is focused in a stage.
FixationBeing stuck in a stage because of unresolved conflicts.
ConflictTension between biological urges and societal expectations.
Oral stageBirth–2 years; mouth; feeding.
Anal stageBirth–2 years; anus; toilet training.
Phallic stage3–5 years; genital area; Oedipus/Electra concepts.
Latency stage6 years–puberty; focus on learning, friends, and social skills.
Genital stagePuberty onward; mature relationships and social interaction.
PubertyPhysical and hormonal changes as a child matures into an adult.
Primary sex characteristicsReproductive organs directly involved in reproduction.
Secondary sex characteristicsPuberty changes driven by sex hormones but not directly involved in reproduction.

The Sexual Self: STDs and Methods of Contraception

PubertyNatural physical and hormonal changes toward adulthood.
Sexual healthKnowledge and practices supporting safety and well-being.
Healthy copingSelf-care and stress management during puberty.
STIsInfections spread mainly through sexual contact.
HPVOften has no symptoms; may cause genital warts and cancer.
Herpes (HSV)Can cause painful blisters; antivirals can reduce recurrences.
SyphilisHas stages and may damage organs if untreated.
Hepatitis B and CCan lead to liver disease and cancer.
TrichomoniasisMay cause genital itching or irritation.
GonorrheaMay cause painful urination and abnormal discharge.
ChlamydiaOften has no symptoms and may affect the reproductive system.
CondomBarrier that helps stop semen entering the uterus and protects against STIs.
Diaphragm / cervical capBarrier covering the cervix.
Birth control pillsHormonal pills that prevent ovulation.
Contraceptive patchReleases hormones through the skin.
Injectable contraceptiveHormonal injection given about every three months.
Contraceptive implantSmall hormone-releasing rod under the skin.
Vaginal ringVaginal device that releases hormones.
Copper IUDIUD that prevents fertilization; can last up to 10 years.
Hormonal IUDIUD releasing progestin; can last 3–7 years.
Fertility awarenessTracking the menstrual cycle to identify fertile days.
WithdrawalRemoving the penis before ejaculation.
Tubal ligationPermanent procedure blocking or cutting fallopian tubes.
VasectomyPermanent procedure blocking or cutting the vas deferens.
Emergency contraceptionTaken after unprotected sex to reduce pregnancy risk.
